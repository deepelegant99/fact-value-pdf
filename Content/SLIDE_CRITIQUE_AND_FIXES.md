# Putnam Fact-Value Deck - Slide Critique and Fix Pass

## Overall critique
The first version had the right argument arc, but several slides were too small or visually thin. The main design problem was not philosophy; it was legibility. Some diagrams used diagonal feedback arrows that looked like accidental scratches, and a few formulas were too cryptic for a first YouTube video. This pass prioritizes readable diagrams, larger conceptual anchors, cleaner feedback loops, and less “math graffiti.”

## Slide-by-slide critique and fixes

| Slide | Critique | Fix applied |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strong hook, but the bottom line was a little cramped and the visual relation between facts/values could be clearer. | Split the hook line into two lines and cleaned the facts/values stack. |
| 2 | The dichotomy was clear, but the formula needed more visual weight. | Enlarged `F ∩ V = ∅` and kept the two-domain setup clean. |
| 3 | Good concept, but the support examples were small and slightly crowded. | Increased node size and simplified the explanatory text. |
| 4 | The bottom formula was too cramped and easy to miss. | Rebuilt the slide around a clearer three-part statement: interaction does not equal conceptual inseparability. |
| 5 | Thick-concept overlap worked, but the circles and central word needed stronger emphasis. | Enlarged the circles and the word `CRUEL`; kept the sentence as the anchor. |
| 6 | Decomposition diagram was good, but the description line was too compressed. | Broke descriptions into two lines inside the factual/evaluative cards. |
| 7 | The feedback arrow looked like it might cut through the explanatory area. | Rerouted the arrow below the main node chain and enlarged the central question. |
| 8 | Too much empty space; the model felt under-explained. | Enlarged the coordinate plane and added a middle explanation near the plotted point. |
| 9 | Example was too small relative to the empty coordinate plane. | Added a larger example card and made the coordinate point more readable. |
| 10 | Same issue as slide 9: too much white space and tiny examples. | Added a larger example card and made the evaluative point clearer. |
| 11 | Thick-region words were small; the interior region was not dominant enough. | Enlarged the entanglement ellipse and labels. |
| 12 | Good transition, but the formula needed to feel like the conceptual punchline. | Enlarged `J_t = (f_t, v_t)` and balanced the timeline spacing. |
| 13 | The values-to-theory diagram was conceptually fine but the lower support text was cramped. | Replaced tiny scattered text with a single “values ask” card plus four compact standards. |
| 14 | The sequence was good but too thin visually. | Enlarged the recurrence formula and tightened labels. |
| 15 | Original feedback arrow looked like a random diagonal slash. Bad. | Replaced it with a straight, deliberate feedback arrow running below the nodes. |
| 16 | Same diagonal-arrow problem as slide 15. It looked accidental. | Replaced the scratch-like arrow with a clean horizontal return arrow and clearer label. |
| 17 | Coupling diagram had too much spaghetti energy; crossing lines were conceptually okay but visually busy. | Routed connectors into and out of a single coupling node so the flow reads cleanly. |
| 18 | Strong ending, but the lower claim was slightly wordy. | Shortened the bottom claim and kept the rationality node dominant. |
| 19 | The notation `F ⟂ V ?` was too cryptic for YouTube. | Replaced it with plain English: `F and V are not independent`. |

## Result
The revised deck is cleaner, more legible, and better suited for narration. It preserves the original intellectual structure: dichotomy → interaction → thick concepts → coordinate model → dynamic feedback → rationality.
